A phone is the one piece of technology most of us use all day, so buy for your real habits rather than the longest list of features. If you spend hours on calls, Facebook and YouTube, battery life and a comfortable screen will matter more than peak performance. If photos are what you care about, compare camera results instead of counting megapixels. And if you play games, look at the processor and how much memory the phone has.

Storage deserves more attention than it usually gets. Apps, photos and videos fill a phone faster than you expect, and not every model lets you add a memory card. Try to leave room for two or three years of use, because moving everything to a new phone later is a hassle.

Also think about how long you plan to keep it. Software updates differ between brands and models, and a phone that keeps getting updates stays safer for longer. Before buying, confirm that the phone supports the network bands used by your operator.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much storage should I get?
Think about how many photos, videos and apps you keep. If you are unsure, choose more than you need today, especially on a phone without a memory card slot.

### Is a higher megapixel camera always better?
No. The sensor, lens and software all matter, so compare actual photos where you can.
