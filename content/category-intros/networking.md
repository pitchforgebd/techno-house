Slow internet is not always your provider's fault. Very often the problem is the router's location, walls that block the signal, or too many devices sharing one old box. Before buying anything, try moving your router somewhere central and away from thick walls. If some rooms still stay weak, then it is time to look at equipment.

For most homes, a good [WiFi router](/category/router) is the place to start. Newer WiFi standards handle many phones and laptops at once more smoothly, but your own devices must support them to get the benefit. In a flat with concrete walls, one router may not reach every room, and a [WiFi range extender](/category/wifi-range-extender) can help. Offices usually need more than that. A [network switch](/category/network-switch) adds wired ports for desktops, printers and cameras, and an [access point](/category/access-point) spreads WiFi evenly over a larger floor. Where security matters, a dedicated [firewall](/category/firewall) gives you more control over the traffic going in and out. For fibre connections, your provider's equipment needs to match your line, so confirm with them before replacing an [ONU](/category/onu).

Wired connections are still the most reliable for a desktop or a console, so run a cable where you can. Each product page lists the exact specification and warranty for that model.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my WiFi weak in some rooms?
Distance and walls are the usual reasons. Move the router to a central spot first, then consider an extender or access point.

### Will a new router make my internet faster?
It can improve coverage and handling of many devices, but it cannot go beyond the speed of the package from your provider.

### Do I need a network switch?
If you have run out of wired ports on your router, a switch adds more of them.
