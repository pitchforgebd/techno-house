A gaming PC is really a set of decisions that have to work together. The graphics card sets how far you can push resolution and settings, the processor keeps frame rates steady in busy games, and the power supply and cooling decide whether the whole thing stays stable through a long session. Getting one part far ahead of the others usually wastes money, so aim for balance.

Start with the games you actually play and the monitor you own or plan to buy. An esports player chasing high refresh rates has different needs from someone who wants a story game to look its best. Our ready-built systems are grouped into [Intel](/category/gaming-pc-intel) and [Ryzen](/category/gaming-pc-ryzen) machines, so you can start from the platform you prefer.

If you would rather choose every part yourself, try the [PC Builder](/pc-builder). It walks you through each slot and checks that the parts fit together before you order. ## Frequently asked questions

### Should I choose Intel or Ryzen for gaming?
Both can make a very good gaming PC. Compare the specific processors at your budget and the games you play, rather than choosing by brand alone.

### Which part matters most for gaming?
Usually the graphics card, as long as the processor is capable enough not to hold it back.

### Can I build my own PC on this site?
Yes. The PC Builder lets you choose parts slot by slot and flags combinations that do not fit together.
