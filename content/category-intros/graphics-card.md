The graphics card does most of the visible work in games, and it also speeds up video editing, 3D rendering and many AI tools. The sensible way to choose one is to start from your monitor. A card that is far stronger than your screen can show is money you will never see on the display, while a weak card will struggle at high resolutions no matter how good the rest of the PC is.

Video memory matters more as you raise resolution and texture quality, but it is only one number. Compare real performance between the specific cards you are considering, because two cards with the same amount of memory can behave very differently.

Before ordering, please check three practical things. First, measure the space inside your [casing](/category/casing), since many modern cards are long and thick. Second, make sure your [power supply](/category/power-supply) has enough capacity and the right power connectors. Third, check that your processor will not hold the card back. ## Frequently asked questions

### How much video memory do I need?
It depends on the games or software you use and your monitor's resolution. Higher resolutions and detailed textures need more.

### Will a graphics card fit in my casing?
Compare the card's length and thickness on its product page with the clearance your casing allows.

### Is my power supply strong enough?
Check the power recommendation for the card and the connectors it needs, then compare those with your power supply. It is wise to leave some headroom.
