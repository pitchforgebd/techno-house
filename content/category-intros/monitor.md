You look at your monitor for hours every day, so it is worth a little thought. Three things shape the experience most: size, resolution and refresh rate. Size has to suit your desk and how far away you sit. Resolution decides how sharp text and images look, and it matters more as screens get larger. Refresh rate decides how smooth motion feels, which gamers notice at once and office users much less.

Panel type also makes a difference. IPS panels are popular because colours stay consistent from different angles, which helps with design and photo work. If games are your priority, look through our [gaming monitors](/category/gaming-monitor) and check that your graphics card can drive the resolution and refresh rate you are paying for. For detailed editing, a [4K monitor](/category/4k-monitor) gives you more working space, and a [curved monitor](/category/curved-monitor) can feel more immersive on wider screens.

Before you buy, look at the ports on your computer and match them to the monitor. HDMI, DisplayPort and USB-C are not interchangeable without the right cable or adapter. ## Frequently asked questions

### What refresh rate do I need?
For office work and browsing, a standard refresh rate is fine. For fast games, a higher refresh rate feels noticeably smoother, as long as your graphics card can keep up.

### Is a bigger monitor always better?
No. A large screen at a low resolution can look less sharp, and it may feel too big if you sit close to it.

### Which cable should I use?
Check which ports both your computer and the monitor have. Use DisplayPort or HDMI versions that support the resolution and refresh rate you want.
