If your computer slows down when you have many browser tabs and programs open at once, memory is often the cause. Adding RAM is one of the simplest upgrades there is, as long as you buy the type your motherboard actually supports.

The first thing to check is the generation. DDR4 and DDR5 modules have different physical designs and cannot replace each other, so a motherboard built for one will not accept the other. Next, look at the speeds your motherboard and processor support. A faster kit may still run, but it can fall back to a lower speed if the system does not support it.

Two matching sticks are usually better than one, because most desktop platforms run memory in dual channel. It is also safest to buy them together as a kit, since mixing different brands and speeds can cause instability. Gaming, video editing and heavy multitasking all benefit from more capacity, while everyday office use needs less.

Tall heat spreaders can get in the way of a large [CPU cooler](/category/cpu-cooler), so check the clearance if your build is tight. This section is for desktop memory. Laptops use a smaller module, so go to [laptop RAM](/category/ram-laptop) for those.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use DDR5 RAM on a DDR4 motherboard?
No. They use different slots, so the RAM must match what your motherboard supports.

### Is it okay to mix different RAM sticks?
It can work, but mixing brands, sizes or speeds may cause problems. A matched kit is the safer choice.

### Why does my RAM show a lower speed than the box says?
Many systems start memory at a default speed. Higher rated speeds often have to be enabled in the BIOS, and your processor and motherboard must support them.
