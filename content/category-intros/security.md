A good security setup begins with a simple walk around your home, shop or office. Note which doors, gates, counters and blind spots really need watching, where you can run cables, and where you can get power. That short list will tell you far more than any catalogue will.

The first decision is usually between analog and IP systems. Analog [CC cameras](/category/cc-camera) record through a [DVR](/category/dvr) and are a familiar, simple choice. [IP cameras](/category/ip-camera) work over a network with an [NVR](/category/nvr) and generally offer more flexibility as a system grows. If you would rather buy everything together, look at a [CC camera package](/category/cc-camera-package) or an [IP camera package](/category/ip-camera-package). For a single room or a rented flat, a [WiFi camera](/category/portable-wifi-camera) can be the easiest option, provided the signal is reliable where you put it.

Think about night vision for dark areas and weather protection for anything outside. For wide spaces, a [PTZ camera](/category/ptz-camera) can pan and zoom to cover more ground. Storage is easy to overlook: decide how many days of footage you want to keep, because the number of cameras and the recording quality decide how large a hard disk you need.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I choose analog or IP cameras?
Analog systems are simple and familiar. IP systems are generally more flexible and easier to expand. The layout of your site and your existing cabling often decide it.

### How long will my recordings be kept?
That depends on hard disk size, number of cameras and recording quality. Older footage is normally overwritten when the disk fills up.

### Can I watch the cameras on my phone?
Many systems support this over the internet. Check the product page, and make sure you change any default password.
