An NVMe SSD is one of the most satisfying upgrades you can make. Windows starts faster, apps open sooner, games load with less waiting, and the drive itself is a small stick with no cables to manage. It connects to an M.2 slot on the motherboard and uses the PCIe interface, which is why it can move data so much faster than an older SATA drive.

Before you order, check that your computer can take one. Not every M.2 slot supports NVMe, and some older slots only accept SATA M.2 drives, so your motherboard or laptop manual is the place to confirm this. Also note which PCIe generation the slot supports. A faster drive will still work in an older slot in most cases, but it will run at the slot's speed, so you may be paying for performance you cannot use.

For capacity, think about what you keep on the drive. Modern games take up a lot of room, and video projects even more. Drives tend to work best when they are not completely full, so leave yourself some space.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will an NVMe SSD work in my laptop?
Only if the laptop has a free M.2 slot that supports NVMe. Check the laptop's manual or its manufacturer's specification first.

### What is the difference between NVMe and SATA SSDs?
Both are far quicker than a hard disk. NVMe uses the faster PCIe connection, so it can transfer data at higher speeds than SATA.

### Do I need a heatsink?
Some motherboards include one, and high-speed drives benefit from it. Thin laptops often have no room for an extra heatsink, so check the space available.
